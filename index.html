Study G Analysis

I believe Study G is a repeated cross-sectional study because it collected data from different random samples of adults each year from 2014 to 2023. The participants only answered questions once about the anxiety symptoms they experienced in the past 30 days.

The study found that anxiety symptoms among adults ages 18 to 25 increased from 8.1% to 14.7%, while anxiety levels among adults ages 50 to 64 remained relatively stable. These findings provide evidence that anxiety became more common among young adults over time.

However, I do not fully agree with the authors' conclusion that anxiety steadily worsened as young adults moved through their twenties. Because the study did not follow the same individuals over the years, it cannot show that specific people became more anxious as they aged.

Therefore, the study supports the idea that anxiety increased among young adults as a group, but it does not prove that individual young adults experienced worsening anxiety as they moved through their twenties.
